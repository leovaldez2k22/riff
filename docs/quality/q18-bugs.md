| Q18-003 | A1 step 4 (owner's report) | Riff Preview 7f7ab8e | Medium | Some songs show line-synced lyrics although word-synced lyrics exist. Check from the computer, 15 Oct 2026: BetterLyrics' public getLyrics for Delicate / Taylor Swift / reputation returned TTML (45,786 bytes) for duration 233 in 0.75 s; for duration 232 the first request did not answer within 25 s and the retry answered in 6.5 s with the same TTML | Read from LyricsRepository.kt, likely two causes together: (1) each provider gets 15 s (`withTimeoutOrNull(15_000)`), and BetterLyrics can take longer on a song's first request, so the app falls through to LRCLIB's line-synced text; (2) whatever is found is cached, and a cached result that is timed at all (line or word) ends the search for good (`if (cached.timed ...) return`), so the line-synced result is never upgraded later even when BetterLyrics would now answer fast. | Q18-004 | A1 step 5 | Riff Preview 7f7ab8e | Low | Some clearly held words do not glow or grow. Example: ABBA, "Dancing Queen", the line "Dancing queen" at 1:36: "queen" is held but stays plain. Letter pop and glow otherwise work | Play "Dancing Queen" (ABBA, word synced, Better Lyrics), open lyrics, watch 1:36 | artifacts/quality/q18a/a1/a1-05-lyrics-word-synced.png. Timing from BetterLyrics' TTML for the song (fetched from the computer, 15 Oct 2026): Dan 1:36.443-1:36.859, cing -1:37.273, queen 1:37.273-1:38.406, so "Dancing" 830 ms (119 ms per letter) and "queen" 1,133 ms (227 ms per letter) | Confirmed by arithmetic against LyricsModels.kt heldLyricWords: a word is held at 1,600 ms or more, or at 900 ms or more when its time per letter is at least 1.5 x the line's median. The median is `perLetter[size / 2]` and includes the word itself; in a two-word line that is the larger value, i.e. "queen" itself (227), so it would need 340 ms per letter. "queen" (1,133 ms) is under 1,600 ms, so it never qualifies. Short lines are where held words are most common. Fix idea: compare a word with the median of the other words of the line (here 119, so 1.9 x) or of the whole song, not with a median that contains it; add this line as a test case | Open |
| Q18-005 | A2 step 4 (owner's report) | Riff Preview 7f7ab8e | Medium | Seeking sometimes stops the sound for too long before it carries on, on streamed **and downloaded** songs; worst when seeking **back** to an earlier part | During playback, drag the seek bar ahead, and back to an earlier part; sometimes playback halts, then resumes after a delay | Owner's report, 18 Oct 2026; no recording or diagnostics yet | Not investigated; read from the code only. Both ExoPlayers (MusicPlaybackService.kt, AudioTransitionCoordinator.kt) use Media3's default load control, which keeps no back buffer, so a seek back in a streamed song discards and downloads again what was already played; a seek ahead past the buffer needs a new range request (possibly a fresh stream URL). A downloaded song is a local file, so a gap there points elsewhere: candidates are a file with no seek index (e.g. WebM/Matroska without Cues, so a seek has to scan the file), the audio pipeline (booster processor, PCM-only output) being flushed and rebuilt, or the main player seeking while crossfade's tail player is prepared. Measure first: time from seek to sound for a streamed and a downloaded song, forward and back, with buffering events from the diagnostics; check the downloaded file's container for a seek index. Fix candidates: a back buffer (setBackBuffer) for streams, cues or a seek index on download, and whatever the measurement shows for local files | Open |
