# Features and behavior

This guide describes Riff 0.10.0 (version code 33), the final candidate of the excellence plan, prepared and not published, as built from this repository on 24 October 2026. The last distributed APK, 0.9.0-rc20 (4 October 2026), predates most of what is new here; see the [release notes](release-notes-0.10.0.md), the [changelog](../CHANGELOG.md) and the [README](../README.md) for availability.

## Browse, search and library

- Real songs can be searched and played; provider availability varies by track and session. Search supports voice input, a clear-text cross icon, title sorting, and pagination.
- Search includes songs, albums, artists, playlists and podcasts. Home and Library collections open real provider details; tapping the player artist opens their profile, with album navigation in the song menu.
- Library reads the signed-in YouTube Music account’s songs, liked songs, playlists, albums, artists and podcasts, with Load more for additional pages and playlist songs. The player star likes/unlikes the song on YouTube Music after the service acknowledges the change.
- Song menus can add a song to several selected YouTube Music playlists. Successfully saved selections are removed from the picker; failed or read-only selections can be retried. Owned playlist rows can remove their exact entry after confirmation. **New playlist** makes a real private YouTube Music playlist; a playlist the account can edit has Playlist options with **Rename** and **Delete** (Delete asks first), and read-only playlists, Liked music and Episodes for later offer neither. A collection's header shows its song count and total length. Local collection search matches title and artist, including accents; sorting supports collection order, title A–Z/Z–A and artist A–Z. Date-added sorting uses supplied dates only: real download commit times are available, while provider collections may omit date metadata.
- Explicit Save on this phone, local playlist tracks and listening history remain account-scoped phone saves. They appear separately from the provider library. Save on this phone is separate from the YouTube Music like and can be undone.
- On-device audio access requests `READ_MEDIA_AUDIO` on Android 13+, or `READ_EXTERNAL_STORAGE` on Android 12, only on that screen. Files remain on the phone.
- Search suggests up to six searches and three ready results while you type (200 ms after the last key; **Settings → Privacy and data → Search suggestions** turns it off). An "All" search whose first result is an artist or album shows it as a large **Top result** card. Empty and offline screens say what to do next; a screen that fails offline offers **Downloads**, which still play.
- Long-press Riff's icon for **Search**, **Shuffle liked songs**, **Downloads** and, while a song is loaded, **Resume**. A Quick Settings tile plays and pauses; with nothing loaded it opens Riff and resumes the saved queue.
- On an artist's page, a shelf that YouTube Music lists in full (albums, singles, videos, top songs) has **See all**. Songs open as a playlist; albums, singles and videos open as a grid that loads more as it scrolls.

- **Encore** (Library → Encore): your listening by day, week, month, year or all time, in a glass period control with a stepper (or a swipe on the headline). Listening time counting up, plays, songs, artists and albums, and the change on the period before; a chart whose bars open their day, month or year; peak hours as a 24-hour clock and a week × hour heatmap; top songs, albums, artists (and genres when on) by minutes or plays, with rank movement; habits (streak, longest session, discovery, finished songs, most repeated, sources); a calendar; milestones; a listening personality with its rule; On this day; Forgotten favourites; Play your top 25, or save them to YouTube Music as a new private playlist; save the top lists as CSV; a weekly goal ring; a long press on a row for Play next and **Your history with this** (also in song menus, Now Playing and album and artist peeks). On Sundays Home shows your week; an opt-in **weekly recap** notification comes on Sunday evening; an **Encore today** home widget; Export includes Encore's totals and top lists. **Stories** open from the ring or a long press on a period: about 6 s a slide, tap sides to move, hold to pause, swipe down to close or sideways for another period, and share any slide as a 1080 × 1920 picture. Everything stays on the phone; **Find genres for your artists** (off by default) asks MusicBrainz about artist names on Wi-Fi.
- **Android Auto:** Riff appears in Android Auto with Home, Library, Downloads and Recent tabs, search and voice ("play … on Riff"). A song plays its list from that song. A sideloaded Riff needs Android Auto's "Unknown sources" developer setting once.
- **Cast:** the output pill under the player lists Chromecasts, Google speakers and TVs on the same Wi-Fi. Choosing one moves the song there at the same place; the notification, lock screen and the volume keys then control the speaker, and the phone keeps the queue. Stopping, or choosing the phone, brings the song back. Songs stored on the phone can't be cast and are skipped. Needs Google Play services.
- **What's new:** after an update, a sheet shows the release's main features once, each with "Try it". A new install shows two tips instead (opening YouTube Music links in Riff, and Riff in Android Auto). Both are in Settings → About → What's new and tips.
- **Large screens:** on tablets, unfolded foldables and wide windows the tabs move to a glass rail; on the widest the player stays open in a pane on the right, with lyrics and the queue inside it.

## Accounts, privacy and data

- Each YouTube Music account, signed-out listening and the audio on the phone have their own queue, history, searches, saved tracks and downloads. Switching accounts saves the outgoing account's queue, stops it, and restores the other account's queue paused; on-device audio keeps playing. A sign-in that YouTube Music does not confirm with a stable account is shown as unverified: saving and downloading say why they are unavailable, and the Account screen offers **Verify account**.
- **Sign-in** ([design](quality/sign-in-design.md)): Riff's frosted screens around Google's own page, which is never restyled. **Welcome back** lists the accounts used on this phone (name, full address, when last used; at most 20, **See all** past three, each removable from the list without touching the Google account). Picking another saved account switches to it directly when Google still holds it in the web session, checked against the account picked; otherwise Google's own account page opens. Riff commits only the account it reached, and never one other than the account picked from its list. **Checking your account** ticks off three steps; **You're in** counts liked songs and playlists and waits for **Start listening**. Sign out asks first and clears the list. Riff never sees the password and keeps no other account's cookies.
- **Settings → Privacy and data**: turn history saving off, keep plays until cleared or for 30, 90 or 365 days, clear an account's or the phone's listening history, clear searches, delete downloads, or delete everything Riff keeps for the account in use. Each action names whose data it removes and how much, asks first, and reports what could not be removed. None changes the YouTube Music account.
- **Earlier data** holds what builds before per-account storage shared between every account (plays, the saved queue, searches) until you import it into the account in use or delete it.
- **Move to another phone** exports chosen owners' settings, saved tracks, history, searches and download names to one unencrypted file you place with the system picker, and imports such a file without removing anything. Android backup and device transfer are disabled. See [privacy](privacy.md). **Backup on this phone** (on by default) keeps one unencrypted file per owner in Documents/Riff Backup, which stays when Riff is uninstalled; a new install offers to restore it after one folder pick, and an account's file is restored when that account signs in. **Not interested** also tells YouTube Music when signed in: its own Not interested where offered, otherwise a dislike, which Undo removes on the same account.

## Home, podcasts and radio

- Home mood backgrounds use licensed real photographs discovered through Openverse and Wikimedia Commons, with daily refresh jobs, a per-mood file cache, creator/licence links and retention of the last successful image offline. The selected image fades into black near the middle of the screen. Missing or failed album/playlist artwork uses one of 20 stable, original minimal covers. Speed dial shows three pages of Listen again songs. Songs, episodes, podcasts, albums, playlists and artists can be pinned from their menus (up to 27, as many as speed dial has tiles); pins come first in the order pinned, carry a badge, are kept on the phone and travel in the export file and the backup.
- Home, Podcasts, Radio and Home mood feeds share requests and cache successful responses in process memory. Tab changes reuse the same data. The cache refreshes at 6 AM and 6 PM in the phone’s local timezone, on removal from recent apps, and on account changes. Failures can be retried. Library checks playlist additions/deletions on every tab entry and retains existing collection contents. Edits invalidate the affected collection.
- Shows for you and podcast shelves live in Podcasts. Radio discovers current broadcasts from 42 curated YouTube channels across lofi, jazz/café, electronic/house, synthwave, ambient/sleep, nature, classical, world, India and news categories, plus Featured and All. Only broadcasts currently marked live appear; channels without a live broadcast are omitted. Live broadcasts use native YouTube HLS in the media service, including attestation, manifest-path token placement and n challenge solving. The full player has app-owned controls and a black video background; minimizing or switching apps detaches the video surface while audio continues. Unavailable streams show an honest retry state. Lyrics preload as playback starts and share one request/result with the lyrics page. Lyrics ask their sources together: word timing from Better Lyrics, Better Lyrics · QQ and NetEase; line timing from Better Lyrics · Kugou, LRCLIB and YouTube Music; plain text from Lyrics.ovh. The first answer shows at once; a better-timed answer (word > line > plain) always replaces it, and one with the same timing from a higher-ranked source only within 2 s of it; sources that can no longer win are cancelled. NetEase verification failures fall through automatically. Credit lines are left out for every source. Word highlighting uses supplied timestamps; songs with only line timing highlight by line, and plain lyrics are labeled honestly.

## Player, queue and widgets

- The notification, lock screen and car show **Shuffle** and, for a YouTube Music song while signed in, **Like** beside the transport buttons. The player menu's **Stats for nerds** shows delivery (SABR, HLS, direct or on the phone), format, codec, bitrate, sample rate, decoder, buffered time, the network estimate and dropouts, refreshed every second, with **Copy**. The back gesture previews where it goes: the player shrinks toward the mini player, a pushed page slides aside (none under reduced motion).
- The full player follows BitChord's layout while keeping Riff's X, NOW PLAYING, shuffle and repeat: the cover melts into a blurred backdrop made from its own colours, the title is larger with the artist under it at 55 %, like and more sit in round buttons, the current lyric line shows above a thin seek bar (tap for the full lyrics; **Settings → Appearance** turns it off), the time row names the song quality, and the output is a pill. **Show stats for nerds** adds the format line over the art.
- The tab bar, search button and mini player are glass: they bend what scrolls behind them at their rims, with a faint colour fringe and a light from the top left. Holding a finger on the tabs lifts a clear glass lens that magnifies the tab under it. The category and filter chips, back buttons, the player's like, more and output buttons, the queue's buttons and toggles, the lyrics page's buttons, menus, dialogs and snack bars, the Settings groups, the equalizer's cards and Dynamic / Manual switcher, and the sign-in panels are glass too; a slider's thumb turns to clear glass while held, and a switch's thumb while it moves. Power saver or a warm phone drops the fringe; a phone about to throttle, and Android 12, draw frosted glass instead; high-contrast controls stay opaque.
- Text is set in Inter 4.1, Display for titles, headings, the song title and sung lyrics, Text for everything smaller; page titles are 34 sp, and Settings rows read larger with their help in grey beneath.
- **Equalizer** (Settings → Sound, or the player menu): Dynamic is a tone pad, left warmer and right brighter, up bringing the middle forward, Broad or Focused; Manual is seven bands from 60 Hz to 14 kHz with presets. A pre-amp keeps the loudest point at 0 dB, balance moves the sound left or right, and the curve is drawn below. Riff's equalizer works before the booster, also in crossfades; the phone's own equalizer is one tap away when it has one.
- Settings → Widgets offers Compact, Wide, Artwork, Player, Large and Circle home-screen widgets. Widget resizing and pinning depend on the launcher. The player menu has preset and custom sleep timers up to 24 hours; the full player displays the remaining time and fades its own volume smoothly during the final 15 seconds before pausing. Cancelling or changing a timer restores the prior player volume; system media volume is unchanged. A running timer keeps its deadline if Android stops the app and is re-armed when playback restarts.
- Settings -> Data usage -> Data saver requests lower-bitrate audio and up to 360p video for newly resolved streams. It is off by default, persists across restarts, and leaves the current stream and download quality unchanged. **Song quality** (High, Medium by default, or Low) chooses what streams ask for; High carries YouTube's Premium hint once Premium is confirmed; Data saver wins while it is on, and downloads always ask for the highest. It is kept per phone and travels in the export file and the backup. Queue rows show a remove button; removing the final song clears the queue and pauses playback.
- The audio-output icon follows the selected route: phone, earphones, headphones or external speakers. The navigation selector springs in both axes and locally refracts icons and labels on Android 13+; Android 12 uses a local elastic fallback. Solid controls and reduced motion disable the refraction.
- The player uses real YouTube Music recommendations for Up next, skips unavailable streams, and supports selecting, reordering, removing, Play next and Add to queue. Albums and playlists load all provider continuation pages, retain collection order, and select the tapped track, with up to 2,000 tracks per collection. Scrolling a song's queue to its end loads more recommendations at once, up to 200, until YouTube has no more. Tapping a song to play it in a list, a collection, Downloads, History or on-device audio opens the full player once it plays; Play and Shuffle do not. **Previous** restarts the song once it has played past 10 seconds, also from the notification, a headset or a widget; a swipe back on the player's artwork always goes to the song before. The player's clock counts hours (1:43:39). Automatic queues reject repeat IDs and matching audio/video recordings; authored repeats retain their positions. The queue supports history, immediate two-bar-handle dragging with edge scrolling, accessibility move actions, and swipe removal. The player no longer offers a song's music video (removed at the user's request in 0.10.0-rc1; live radio still plays its video).
- Background audio continues while switching apps, and stops when this app is removed from recent apps, after saving the current song, position and queue for a paused next launch. Player/lyrics share song and artist motion; the mini player retains stable layout bounds. Both seek and volume use continuous capsule tracks with no floating thumb. Animations request the supported 60, 90 or 120 Hz UI rate without changing system display settings; refresh rate does not guarantee 120 frames per second.

## Downloads, storage and cache

- Playlist and album pages offer Download all songs once every collection page has loaded. Bulk downloads include eligible songs in the full collection regardless of the current search or sort, deduplicate repeated songs, and keep completed downloads. Downloads offers Delete all downloads with confirmation; it removes audio, artwork and lyrics and cancels pending transfers for the current account. Clearing playback cache also requires confirmation and keeps downloaded bundles. Unlimited removes the app budget limit while preserving the device storage reserve.
- A song menu offers Download song, or Remove download (pink, with a trash can) when the song is already downloaded or on its way; every song menu, including the home page's long-press menu and the player's menu, decides this the same way. Download song saves original source audio at the highest available quality, artwork and timed lyrics when available. Library → Downloads uses a full artwork header from the most recently downloaded song, Play and Shuffle controls, and compact rows with local title/artist search and date/title/artist sorting. Playlists omit track numbers; album and playlist titles truncate to one line. Collection row playback preserves the original authored queue and selected index despite filtering or sorting. Its ordered queue includes ready downloads only. Progress, pause, retry/keep, details and removal remain available; offline playback bypasses extraction and permanent bundles have no cache expiry. Downloads are app-private and account-scoped; clearing the playback cache keeps them. Uninstalling deletes them. Failed transfers restart safely with fresh exact-ID streams; container remuxing preserves audio quality and adds seek indexing. Lyrics and artwork failures are shown separately.
- Settings → Smart Downloads enables up to 250 recent qualifying songs, off by default. Membership uses recency and meaningful listening; repeats, completion and favorites rank priority. Live broadcasts, podcasts, songs over 20 minutes, brief listens and user-removed downloads are excluded. Automatic work uses unmetered Wi-Fi by default; Settings → Downloads → Allow mobile data explicitly permits validated mobile or metered connections. Both scheduled jobs and active transfers honor that choice. Work requires battery ≥20%, a storage budget from 1–16 GB in 1 GB steps, then 24, 32, 64, 128, 256 GB and Unlimited (default 4 GB), and keeps a tenth of device storage free, at least 1 GB and at most 2 GB. Playback takes priority. Manual downloads are preserved when Smart Downloads is disabled or its rolling selection changes. The number downloaded can be below 250 because of available listening history, storage, eligibility or provider failures. Android schedules background work; enabling the toggle does not promise immediate completion.

## Lyrics, appearance and interaction

- Word-synced lyrics light each word with a white sweep as it is sung. Lines that overlap (two voices at once) are lit together, and the view follows the earliest of them. Background vocals, where the lyrics mark them (Better Lyrics TTML), are a smaller line under the main one with their own timing. A held word (at least 1.6 s, or 0.9 s and much slower per letter than the rest of its line) grows about 7%, rises slightly and glows pink while it is sung, and its letters shimmer very slightly until it ends; neighbouring words do not move, and a held word broken across two display lines is not grown. Each letter of a word-synced line pops up 1 dp as the sweep reaches it and stays up until the line leaves the screen. Words in scripts whose letters join (Arabic, Devanagari and the like) pop and shake as a whole. With Android's animations turned off (Remove animations, or animator duration scale off), nothing pops, grows or shakes; held words still glow. Line-synced and plain lyrics have no word timing, so none of this applies to them, and overlap needs end times that LRC and YouTube Music lyrics rarely give.
- Hold a lyric line for 650 ms to share: pick up to five lines above or below it, in a cover, dark, Riff pink or Aurora look, then **Save to Pictures/Riff** or **Share**; a tap on a timed line still seeks to it. Punctuation lights with its word.
- Lyrics timing starts and resets at +0.2s. Timing and option menus use a blurred backdrop with sharp controls. Appearance has separate Glass intensity and Frosted glass intensity sliders; frosted glass is on by default at 20 %, covers the glass to its edge and bends at the rim. High-contrast controls use opaque backgrounds. Settings are grouped in compartments (Sound, Look & feel, Downloads & data, Library & privacy, Widgets, About) that open one at a time. Tapping the queue thumbnail returns to the player using its artwork transition.
- Haptics use Android semantic touch feedback, respect the system touch setting, and can be disabled in Settings. The boost, crossfade and glass sliders tick as they move: the boost strongest at 100, 200 and 300 %, lighter at 150 and 250 %, lightest at the quarter steps; crossfade at each second; glass every 25 %. Routine scrolling, typing, background work and automatic song changes remain silent. Double tapping Home, Podcasts, Radio or Library resets its own root and scroll state; Search also focuses the field and keyboard.
- Lines that aren't being sung are very slightly blurred, more the further they are from the sung one, and the lyrics fade out at the top and bottom of the page. The blur stops while you scroll the lyrics yourself, for unsynced lyrics and under Reduce motion; Settings → Appearance → Blur other lyric lines turns it off.
- During an instrumental stretch of 4 seconds or more (an intro, a solo, a break between verses), three dots fill one after another until the singing returns, where the lyrics say when each line ends. Tapping them plays from the start of the stretch.
- Lyrics can also come from lrc.red, which often has word timing.
- For lyrics in another script (Hindi, Japanese, Korean, Russian and others), a chip under the lyrics switches between the original, a romanized form and both. The romanization comes from the lyrics source when it has one, otherwise the phone makes one ("Auto").
- Song quality has separate choices **On Wi-Fi** and **On mobile data** (Settings → Playback); a phone hotspot counts as mobile data.
- The sleep timer can stop at the **end of this song**, **after 2, 3 or 5 songs** or at the **end of the queue**, and its fade-out lasts off, 5, 15, 30 or 60 seconds.
- **Playback speed** (player menu) goes from 0.5× to 2× without changing pitch. Episodes keep their speed; songs return to normal when Riff restarts.
- YouTube Music and YouTube links can be shared to Riff, or opened in it once allowed in Android's settings (Settings → Playback → Open YouTube Music links in Riff): songs, videos, playlists, albums and artists, with a start time when the link has one.
- Removing a song from the queue offers **Undo**, which puts it back where it was; removing one from your playlist offers Undo too, which adds it back at the end.
- In the full player, a song title too long for its line scrolls slowly after a pause. The mini player, lists and the queue keep "…".
- Screens move at a calm pace (Settings → Appearance → Calm transitions; off makes them quicker). Things that follow your finger or the music keep their own timing.
- The top and bottom of each screen fade to slightly dark, under the status bar and behind the floating bar.
- Over a light cover in the full player, the status bar's clock and icons, the close button and "NOW PLAYING" turn dark so they stay readable.

## Automatic crossfade

Enable **Settings → Audio → Crossfade** and select a duration from **1–12 seconds**. Visual fades occur only during eligible automatic audio handoffs, for the entire live blend. Manual selection or skipping replaces the song presentation immediately.

- Crossfade prepares the outgoing audio copy up to 30 seconds before the blend and preloads up to 12 seconds of the next track. Audio with a null video URI remains eligible. Artwork, title and artist follow the live audio blend in the full player, mini player and queue header for its entire duration, respecting reduced motion. Manual song changes and crossfade-off playback replace content immediately. Both gains use one monotonic equal-power ramp; decoder startup and buffering hold progress without compressing the duration, and delayed ticks are bounded to prevent volume jumps. The outgoing copy uses matching loudness processing. Pauses, seeks and disabling crossfade cancel it; live/video items, repeat-one and active sleep timers do not blend.
- **Smart crossfade** (off by default) reads the end and start of downloaded and on-device songs: the blend starts where the song winds down and ends with its last sound, 4–12 seconds, the next song skips a short quiet intro, and halfway through the bass of one song is swapped for the other's. When both songs have a steady beat, the blend starts on a downbeat of the outgoing song (the first of a four-bar phrase where one is near) and the next song comes in on its own downbeat. Streams keep the set timing with the bass swap; an album played in order blends plainly.
- **Skip silence at the end of songs** (off by default) moves on once a song has been silent for 1.5 seconds near its end; **Skip all silence** trims every gap, also inside songs.

## Playback reliability

- **Settings → Normalize volume**, off by default, turns down songs louder than YouTube Music's reference level (by at most 12 dB) when it supplies loudness data, also offline for songs downloaded with this version. It never turns a song up, and audio from the phone's own library is unchanged. The **Volume booster** and its level are separate.

Ordinary online audio now uses direct transport. Playback cache identities distinguish transport, format and bitrate, addressing the short playback interruption without changing the selected quality. See [the 5 October continuity verification](playback-fix-2026-10-05.md).

- Playback starts from the selected song's metadata without resolving the remaining queue. Lyrics and recommendations start after playback is ready and playing. The shared provider prewarms extraction and coalesces requests with a bounded, expiring RAM stream cache. Persistent catalog/listening metadata and a 256 MB LRU audio cache support repeat listening. Signed URLs and tokens are not written into download metadata. Partial playback cache is an optimization; use Download song for guaranteed offline storage. Highest available does not imply a fixed bitrate or lossless audio.
- YouTube challenge solving (QuickJS) runs in a separate `:solver` process, so a crash there doesn't stop the music. If the app itself crashes, the queue comes back paused with a one-time "Stopped unexpectedly" line in the mini player (TalkBack reads the full sentence); playback never resumes on its own after a crash. If the network drops, playback resumes by itself when it returns. Settings → About → Diagnostics shows how Riff last closed (a crash, not responding, closed for memory, or an ordinary stop) and the errors it recorded, and can share or copy a cleaned report with the build, those records and the recent playback event log (no URLs, tokens, cookies, titles or messages) for bug reports. If YouTube Music stops accepting the sign-in, Home and Library show "Session expired · Sign in again".

## Accessibility

- Every queue row offers **Move up**, **Move down**, **Play next** and **Remove** to a screen reader or a switch, and says its position; the like button says whether the song is liked. Pressable controls take presses in at least 48 dp.
- Screens are checked at font scales up to 2.0, right to left, landscape and a large window; at large text on a short screen the player scrolls. Words over a photograph on Home sit on a scrim dark enough for a white picture; album, playlist, artist and Downloads headers and the player use 0.9.0-rc20's artwork colours and fades at the owner's choice, so on a pale cover some words read below 4.5:1 ([accessibility](quality/accessibility.md)). **High-contrast controls**, **Reduce motion** and solid controls are in Settings. Text is English only.
- No formal accessibility audit has been done, and no TalkBack or keyboard walk-through was done on a phone (the owner's decision); each download phase is announced once (Q18-031), tested in code only.

## Updates

- Riff never downloads or installs updates. A build made with a release feed can ask it once a day whether a newer version exists and show one notice per version that opens the release page (Settings → About → Check for updates, on by default). **This build has no feed**, because no release is published, so it shows no such setting and makes no request ([update notice](quality/update-notice.md)).
