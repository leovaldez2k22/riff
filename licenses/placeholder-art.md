# Riff placeholder cover artwork

Twenty original raster covers were generated with the built-in OpenAI image_gen tool for Riff on 2026-10-02. They are fallback assets for absent or failed provider cover requests; valid provider art takes precedence. Each collection selects a stable cover from its identifier.

The user requested minimal artwork with visible design. The final direction uses layered curves, folds, restrained colour, grain and negative space. The images contain no typography or borrowed artist identity. Each final asset is a 512 × 512 WebP in `app/src/main/res/drawable-nodpi/cover_fallback_01.webp` through `cover_fallback_20.webp`; their combined encoded size is 331,184 bytes.

Generation used one tool call per cover. The first image established the style; later calls used that image as a style reference with the following concepts. The original PNGs, contact sheet and generation metadata are retained in the local release evidence directory `artifacts/features-rc15/placeholder-art-minimal/`.

## Style prompt

Create a premium square album-cover placeholder for the Riff music app. Minimal, but visibly designed: a flowing sculptural ribbon made from three layered paper-like curved bands in muted dusty rose, warm cream and deep burgundy, crossing with thoughtful asymmetry through the middle of a near-black plum background. Fine subtle grain, soft dimensional shadows, crisp elegant silhouette, restrained composition with about 40 percent negative space, strong visual identity readable at small thumbnail size. Contemporary editorial music artwork, polished and understated. No text, no letters, no logos, no musical note symbols, no border, no UI, no people. Opaque square image. It should feel like a complete designed cover, not a lone dot or simple line.

## Daily Home photographs

Home photographs are discovered through Openverse, with Wikimedia Commons as a fallback. The app accepts CC0, Public Domain, CC BY and CC BY-SA photography and retains the creator, licence and source links. A Photo credits action accompanies the displayed photograph. Openverse is an index, not the author of the individual photos. The app preserves the last successfully fetched photograph when the network or source is unavailable.
